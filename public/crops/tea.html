Camellia sinensis

Tea (Camellia sinensis) Cultivation & TRA Protection Guide

North Bengal & Dooars/Terai smallholder tea guide: acidic pH drainage, Albizia shade management, plucking rounds, Red Spider Mite & Looper IPM.

Last Reviewed & Updated: 2026-11-18

Agronomic Profile

Season: Main Plucking Flush: March – November (Winter Pruning Dec–Jan)

Soil: Acidic sandy loam to clay loam (pH 4.5 – 5.5) with deep sub-surface drainage

Climate: Humid subtropical, 18°C – 30°C with well-distributed rainfall

Harvest Cycle: 7–10 day plucking rounds (Two leaves and a bud)

Organic & Preventive Practices

Follow Tea Board Plant Protection Code (PPC): prune affected deadwood, apply neem/azadirachtin formulations, conserve natural predators, and observe strict post-spray plucking intervals.

Cultivation Guide

  1. Test soil acidity annually; maintain optimal pH 4.5–5.5 and keep lateral drains clean before monsoon.
  2. Maintain recommended shade canopy (Albizia odoratissima / Indigofera) to regulate leaf temperature and reduce mite stress.
  3. Follow Tea Board / TRA pruning and skiffing calendar during winter dormancy to sustain plucking table health.
  4. Pluck selectively ('two leaves and a bud') every 7–10 days for superior made-tea liquor and bush vigor.

Major Pests & Diseases

  • Red Spider Mite (Oligonychus coffeae): Reddish bronze specks along midrib on upper leaf surface in dry sunny pockets.
  • Tea Mosquito Bug (Helopeltis): Black necrotic punctures on tender buds causing curling and shoot dieback.
  • Looper Caterpillar & Thrips: Defoliate flush leaves or cause sand-paper streaks underneath leaves.
Safety Notice: Always verify pesticide registration, active ingredients, and exact per-hectare label dosages on official CIBRC (cibrc.gov.in) product labels or consult your nearest BCKV / UBKV / KVK agronomist before spraying. Never exceed recommended Waiting Periods (PHI).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal soil pH range for North Bengal small tea gardens?

Camellia sinensis thrives in acidic soil between pH 4.5 and 5.5 with zero waterlogging.

Why does Red Spider Mite flare up after dusty dry spells?

Dust accumulation on tea leaves shields mites from predators and warms leaf microclimate; washing sprays and shade management suppress outbreaks.

Where should small tea growers verify permitted tea chemicals?

Only use molecules listed in the official Tea Board of India Plant Protection Code (PPC) and CIBRC guidelines.
