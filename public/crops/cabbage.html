Brassica oleracea var. capitata

Cabbage (Banda Kopi) Cultivation & Pest Guide

Cole crop cultivation guide covering boron nutrition, Mustard trap crop for Diamondback Moth, Black Rot drainage, and compact head harvest.

Last Reviewed & Updated: 2026-11-18

Agronomic Profile

Season: Rabi / Winter (Sept – Feb)

Soil: Well-drained loamy soil rich in organic matter (pH 6.0 – 6.8)

Climate: 15°C – 22°C for compact head formation

Harvest Cycle: 70 – 95 days after transplanting

Organic & Preventive Practices

Indian Mustard trap crop strips, Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) biopesticides per label, and clean field sanitation.

Cultivation Guide

  1. Transplant 25–30 day hardened seedlings at 45 cm × 45 cm spacing on well-prepared beds.
  2. Avoid excess nitrogen late in head formation which causes loose or split heads.
  3. Ensure adequate Boron and Calcium availability to prevent hollow stem and internal tip burn.

Major Pests & Diseases

  • Diamondback Moth (Plutella xylostella): Small green larvae feeding on leaf windows; grow 2 rows of Indian Mustard every 25 rows as a trap crop.
  • Aphids & Clubroot: Manage soil pH above 6.0 and rotate cole crops with non-brassicas.
  • Black Rot: V-shaped yellow lesions at leaf margins.
Safety Notice: Always verify pesticide registration, active ingredients, and exact per-hectare label dosages on official CIBRC (cibrc.gov.in) product labels or consult your nearest BCKV / UBKV / KVK agronomist before spraying. Never exceed recommended Waiting Periods (PHI).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do cabbage heads crack before harvest?

Sudden heavy irrigation or rain after a dry spell causes heads to absorb water rapidly and split; harvest promptly at maturity.

How does Mustard protect cabbage from Diamondback Moth?

Mustard attracts Diamondback moths away from cabbage heads when planted along border strips.

What triggers hollow stem inside cabbage?

Rapid growth under high nitrogen combined with Boron deficiency causes internal stem hollowing.
